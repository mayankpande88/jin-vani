translation_en: 'Wrong belief, non-restraint, the passions and activity are both conscious (*saṁjña*) and non-conscious (*asaṁjña*). Those of many kinds that occur in the soul are the soul&apos;s own non-separate modifications (*ananya-pariṇāma*).'

meaning_en: |
  The *Āsrava-adhikāra* (gathas 164–180) opens with this gatha. Kundakunda names four conditions (*pratyaya*) through which karma flows in: wrong belief (*mithyātva*), non-restraint (*avirati*), the passions (*kaṣāya*), and activity of body, speech and mind (*yoga*).

  He then makes a distinction that is easy to miss. Each of the four exists in two forms. As non-conscious (*asaṁjña*) factors they are karmic matter; the next gatha (165) says these are what cause new knowledge-obscuring and other karmas. As conscious (*saṁjña*) states they occur in the soul and are the soul&apos;s own modifications, not separate from it. And what makes the material factors bind at all is the soul that produces attachment and aversion.

  Amṛtacandra draws the conclusion in the *Ātmakhyāti*: in truth, attachment, aversion and delusion (*rāga, dveṣa, moha*) are the āsrava, and they occur only in one who lacks knowledge. He opens the chapter with a verse picturing knowledge as the invincible archer who defeats āsrava.

  A note on lists. The Tattvartha Sutra (8.1) gives *five* causes of bondage, adding carelessness (*pramāda*). The Samayasara counts four, here and again at gatha 109, where the same four are called the general causes of bondage.

Four doors, each with two sides: a material side through which karma comes in, and a conscious side that is the soul&apos;s own doing. Kundakunda places the hinge on the conscious side.
