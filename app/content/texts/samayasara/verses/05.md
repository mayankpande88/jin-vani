  समयसार के पुण्य-पाप अधिकार (गाथा १४५–१६३) की केन्द्रीय शिक्षा। अधिकार की पहली गाथा (१४५) प्रश्न उठाती है: अशुभ कर्म को कुशील और शुभ कर्म को सुशील कहा जाता है — पर जो संसार में ही प्रवेश कराता है, वह सुशील कैसे हो सकता है? यह गाथा उत्तर देती है: सोने की बेड़ी और लोहे की बेड़ी — दोनों बाँधती हैं। उसी प्रकार शुभ और अशुभ — दोनों कर्म जीव को बाँधते हैं। आचार्य अमृतचन्द्र (आत्मख्याति) कहते हैं: बन्ध-रूप होने में दोनों में कोई अन्तर नहीं।

  इसीलिए अगली गाथा (१४७) कहती है — इन दोनों कुशीलों से न राग करो, न संसर्ग। मोक्ष पुण्य के संचय से नहीं होता; उसका कारण ज्ञान है, जैसा आगे गाथा १५१ में आता है।

translation_en: 'Just as an iron fetter binds a man, and a golden fetter binds him too, so karma that has been done, whether auspicious or inauspicious, binds the soul.'

meaning_en: |
  This is the most-quoted teaching from the *Puṇya-pāpa* chapter (gathas 145–163) — and one of the more counterintuitive moves in Jain philosophy.

  Common moral thinking treats merit (*puṇya*) as good and demerit (*pāpa*) as bad — and assumes the path is to accumulate merit and avoid demerit. The chapter opens (gatha 145) by asking how good karma can be called &ldquo;good conduct&rdquo; at all when it, too, leads the soul into saṁsāra. This gatha answers with an image. The merit-soul is in golden chains; the demerit-soul is in iron chains. The chains differ in appearance and in their immediate effects, but they are both *chains*. Amṛtacandra&apos;s *Ātmakhyāti* puts it in one line: as bondage, the two are not different.

  Liberation therefore requires going beyond *both*. The next gatha (147) draws the practical conclusion: form no attachment to either kind of karma, and keep no company with it. What does lead to nirvāṇa, the chapter goes on to say (gatha 151), is knowledge — the soul abiding in its own nature.

  This is theologically demanding. It says that even good karma binds. Liberation is not the maximization of good karma; it is the transcendence of the entire karma-binding mechanism.

The Puṇya-pāpa chapter delivers the verse that surprises every modern reader: even good karma binds. Liberation is not heaping up merit. It is moving beyond the entire merit-and-demerit transaction.
