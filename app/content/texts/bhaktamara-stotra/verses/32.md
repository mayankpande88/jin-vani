translation_en: 'Filling every quarter with deep, far-carrying sound; expert at drawing together the beings of the three worlds for the auspicious assembly; announcing the victory of the king of true *dharma* — the *dundubhi* drum sounds in the sky, proclaiming Your glory.'

meaning_en: 'Verse 32 names the fifth prātihārya in the stotra — the *dundubhi*, the divine drum that sounds in the sky during the *samavasarana*. The drum&apos;s function in Jain iconography is to announce to all three worlds that a Tirthankara is teaching. This verse and the next three (33, 34, 35) belong to the Digambara recension and are absent from the 44-verse Shvetambara recension. From verse 36 onward, Digambara verse numbers therefore run four ahead of Shvetambara numbers (Digambara 36 = Shvetambara 32, through Digambara 48 = Shvetambara 44).'

Verses 32–35 are the four verses found in the Digambara recension and absent from the Shvetambara one. Together with verses 28–31 (the aśoka tree, lion-throne, fly-whisks and three umbrellas) they complete the eight prātihāryas: the dundubhi (divine drum), the puṣpa-vṛṣṭi (rain of flowers), the bhāmaṇḍala (halo) and the divya-dhvani (divine voice).
